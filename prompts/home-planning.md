You are a senior product manager and UX designer.

Your task is to write a clear, practical product specification for a feature in a personal finance app called "FinPilot".

---

## 🎯 Feature Overview

Feature name: Home Planning (MVP – Form-based)

This feature helps users in Vietnam create a simple and realistic plan to buy a house.

The experience is based on a short multi-step form (not chatbot), designed to feel easy, fast, and not overwhelming.

---

## 🧩 Product Context

- FinPilot helps young people plan their financial goals
- Main target users: 22–40 years old, working professionals in Vietnam
- Many users feel:
  - Buying a house is too complicated
  - They don’t know how much they can afford
  - They are afraid of making the wrong financial decision

This feature should reduce anxiety and give users clarity.

---

## 🎯 Goal of the Feature

Help users answer 3 key questions:

1. “Can I afford to buy a house?”
2. “How much house should I aim for?”
3. “What should I do from now to get there?”

---

## 🧠 User Flow

1. User starts the experience
2. User goes through a short step-by-step form (4–5 steps max)
3. Each step asks simple, easy-to-answer questions
4. At the end, the system shows a personalized home-buying plan

---

## 🧩 What to Include in the Specification

Write a structured product spec that includes:

---

### 1. Problem & User Insight

- What problem users are facing
- Why this problem matters
- Emotional aspect (confusion, fear, uncertainty)

---

### 2. Target Users

- Who this feature is for
- Their typical financial situation and mindset

---

### 3. User Experience Design

Describe the full experience in detail:

- How the form is structured (number of steps, flow)
- What kind of questions are asked in each step
- Tone of the UI (friendly, simple, non-judgmental)
- How to reduce friction:
  - Avoid overwhelming users
  - Provide suggestions instead of forcing exact answers
- How to handle:
  - Users who don’t know the answer
  - Users who feel unsure

---

### 4. Information Collected

Clearly list all information the system needs from the user, grouped logically.

Example groups:

- Goal (time, type of house)
- Income & expenses
- Savings
- Household situation
- Loan preference

---

### 5. Output Experience (Very Important)

Describe what the user sees at the end:

- A clear and simple plan that answers:
  - Suitable house price range
  - Required upfront money
  - Monthly savings needed
  - Whether they should consider a loan
- The explanation must feel:
  - Practical
  - Easy to understand
  - Reassuring (not scary or too technical)

---

### 6. UX Principles

Define key principles such as:

- Simplicity over completeness
- Clarity over accuracy
- Guidance over calculation
- Reduce anxiety, increase confidence

---

### 7. Edge Cases (User Perspective)

Describe how the experience should behave when:

- User doesn’t know their numbers
- User inputs unrealistic expectations
- User has very low income or savings

Focus on UX handling, not technical solutions.

---

### 8. Success Criteria

Define how we know this feature works:

- Users complete the form
- Users understand their situation better
- Users feel more confident about buying a house
- Users are willing to continue (e.g., save or sign up)

---

## 🧱 Constraints

- Keep it simple enough for an MVP
- Avoid complexity or advanced features
- Focus on real user needs, not perfect financial accuracy

---

## ✨ Output Format

- Clear markdown
- Well-structured sections
- Use bullet points where helpful
- Avoid technical terms

---

## 🎯 Goal

The final output should be understandable by:

- Product managers
- Designers
- Founders

It should clearly describe what to build and why, without any technical implementation details.
